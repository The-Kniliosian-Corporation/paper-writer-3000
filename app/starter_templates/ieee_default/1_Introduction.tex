\section{Introduction}
Paragraph 1 (Broad context): open with the wider importance of the topic.
Describe the general area of software engineering and why it matters for
research or for industrial practice. Keep this paragraph short.

Paragraph 2 (Existing work): summarize what prior studies have established
on this topic. Focus on the line of work that this paper builds on or
challenges. Two to four citations are usually enough here.

Paragraph 3 (Gap): explain what prior work does not cover, or where its
limitations lie. Be specific. A vague gap weakens the rest of the paper.

Paragraph 4 (This paper): state the goal, list the research questions at a
high level, and describe the methodology in one or two sentences. Avoid
giving numerical results in the introduction unless they are the headline
contribution.

Paragraph 5 (Contributions and outline): list the main contributions as a
bullet list, then give a short paragraph that outlines the structure of
the paper.

Tip: if the introduction is hard to write before the study is finished,
skip it and return after Sections III to V are drafted.

Introduction should be more than 1 page and less than 1.5 pages.
